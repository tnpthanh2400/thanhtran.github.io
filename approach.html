How I think

I don’t optimize metrics. I diagnose the funnel.

The metric is not the strategy. I look for the pattern between reach, engagement, progression, opportunity, pipeline and revenue — then decide what the system needs next.

Interactive diagnostic

Pick the symptom.

This is how I frame the next question instead of jumping straight to a tactic.

Growth problem → operating method

Give me a pipeline problem — here’s how I attack it.

Example: “We need more enterprise pipeline.” I turn the statement into a measurable problem, locate the constraint, then build the smallest useful test.

01

Define

How much? By when? Which segment? What business outcome?

02

Diagnose

Market, coverage, awareness, engagement, conversion or Sales follow-up?

03

Segment

ICP, account potential, behavior, intent, journey and history.

04

Form a hypothesis

What do I believe is preventing growth?

05

Design the test

Audience, message, offer, channel and Sales play.

06

Instrument

Tracking, CRM, campaign structure and dashboard.

07

Launch + learn

Measure leading and downstream indicators.

08

Scale or change

Scale what works. Fix or stop what does not.

Measurement lens

Different stages answer different questions.

Optimization is diagnosis → learning → reallocation → scale. I connect direct, influenced and progression signals rather than forcing every decision through one last touch.

Awareness

Reach + coverage

Are we reaching enough of the right market and accounts?

Engagement

Interaction

Are the audience and message generating meaningful response?

Progression

Movement

Are qualified accounts moving forward through the journey?

Revenue

Opportunity + pipeline

Are downstream outcomes improving — and where is the friction?

My range

Strategic enough to design it. Hands-on enough to build it.
